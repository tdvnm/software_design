The idea, in five sentences

Introduction. At Krea's School of Interwoven Arts and Sciences a student takes a major from one of thirteen subjects and may add a second major, a minor or a concentration on top of it, and most students make that second decision somewhere in year one or year two, after they have seen what the subjects are actually like.

Related work. To make it they have the timetable spreadsheet the academic office circulates before each trimester, a credit table on each subject's page, one mentor meeting, and whatever seniors remember, while universities that run a student information system answer the same question with degree-audit software such as Workday Student, Ellucian Degree Works or Stellic.

Problem. Every major and minor has a last trimester by which it has to be started, because its required courses sit in a prerequisite chain and most of them run in only one trimester a year: a mathematics major has to begin with MATH191 in the third trimester of year one or the chain MATH230, MATH231, MATH332, MATH333, MATH434 no longer fits before graduation, a biology minor has to begin in the first trimester of year one, sociology can be picked up as late as the eighth, and not one of those dates appears in any document the university publishes.

Solution. Tracey will compute that date, out of a catalogue rebuilt from the seven published timetables into 474 courses with their offering trimesters, year eligibility and a prerequisite graph, joined to the requirement sets on the thirteen programme pages, and a walk over that graph that gives the earliest trimester each required course can be taken, which turns into a plain answer: the last trimester you can still declare this combination, and the course that breaks first if you miss it.

Validation. Replay two past cohorts' registrations and see whether the students who declared after the computed deadline are the ones who dropped the second major, overloaded a trimester or graduated a term late, and show each department's computed deadline to the mentors who already advise on it and ask where it is wrong.

The full proposal, with the one-page summary and the Business Model Canvas, is in the documents below.

What this prototype does

The planner is the first small slice of that idea, built with plain HTML, TypeScript and Sass, native browser modules, and Node's built-in HTTP server and SQLite.

  • Major and minor selectors that label the plan. Programme requirements are not checked.
  • A catalogue of the 474 real courses, searchable by code or title and filterable by subject.
  • Course details: code, title, subjects, credits and description.
  • A year-by-trimester table: pick a trimester, add a course, remove it again.
  • Credit totals per trimester and overall, and no course can be added twice.

The plan lives in the browser and resets on reload. Prerequisite checks, the declaration deadline, hints, saved plans and sign-in are deliberately not built yet.

Components from the design, and where they stand

Assignment 2 laid out the full system as eight components. This is how much of each the prototype contains.

Component Responsibility Runs In this prototype
Client Subject pickers, a course catalogue, a year × trimester grid, a details panel. Browser Yes, simplified. Ordinary buttons and selects instead of drag-and-drop.
API The contract between browser and server: catalogue read, plan create/read/update/delete. Server GET /api/health only. Plan routes are documented, not built.
Catalogue store Courses, subjects and the links between them. Database Yes: three tables filled by npm run db:import.
Plan store One student, several named plans, each a set of (course, year, trimester) placements. Database Tables exist; nothing writes to them yet.
Ingest pipeline Read the timetables and programme pages, resolve prerequisites, write the catalogue. Batch Not started. The CSV is a one-off export of that work.
Deadline engine The last trimester a programme can still be declared, and the course that breaks first. Server Not started.
Rule engine Credit floors and caps, prerequisite order, offering trimester, eligibility, in plain words. Shared Not started.
Identity Sign-in with the university account, sessions, read-only share links. Server Not started.
